How this site was made

A short account of who this is for, how the content was researched and checked, and the choices we made about AI, privacy, and accessibility — so that every decision can be inspected.

1 · The brief 2 · Method & AI 3 · Trust & care

The brief

Audience Hong Kong university students, roughly 18–24, who may be stressed but not recognise the signals or feel able to seek help.
Objective Help readers recognise common patterns of stress, reduce hesitation about reaching out, and connect them to verified support — without diagnosing or labelling.
Main message Stress often shows up in small, ordinary ways. Reaching out is a normal, brave step — not weakness, and not a diagnosis.
In scope Fictional narrative, cited local evidence, support pathways, stated limits, method and AI log.
Out of bounds No diagnosis, no treatment advice, no personal health data, no invented statistics, no real patient stories.

Method & AI

1. Evidence gathering

We used Hong Kong-specific, aggregate, non-identifiable sources: the 2025 CUHK & Mental Health Association Depression Index Survey (N=2,695), a 5.5-year cohort study of local university students (Pan et al., 2025, N=960), and Department of Health figures. No personal data was collected, scraped, or analysed. Every health claim was checked against a named source; where a figure's denominator or date could not be verified, it was left out.

2. Story construction

Jay is a fictional composite built from common patterns in the local research — poor sleep, trouble focusing, social withdrawal, and hesitation to seek help. He is not based on any real person. Choices never produce a score or label; each opens a short, non-judgemental note about the signal it represents.

3. Keeping story and evidence apart

The story and the statistics sit on separate pages and are visually distinguished. Jay is explicitly labelled fictional. Each statistic carries its source, population, measure, and denominator. The story is never presented as evidence, and the numbers are never used to explain an individual.

Trust & care

Privacy

The site collects no personal data: no names, emails, phone numbers, or health information. There is no login, no tracking cookie, and no identifying analytics — it is static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. External support links open in a new tab, where that organisation's privacy policy applies.

Ethics

  • Person-first language. Jay is a person experiencing stress — not "an anxious person" or "a depressive."
  • No crisis tropes. The story shows an ordinary, relatable day; recovery is not a single dramatic turning point.
  • Honest uncertainty. We state what we cannot say: screening is not diagnosis, correlation is not causation, and 2021 data is not today's prevalence.
  • No over-promising. This site cannot diagnose, treat, or cure; it points to verified professional support instead.
  • Opt-in media. Ambient sound is off by default, so no one is startled by audio they did not ask for.

Accessibility

  • Skip-to-content link and semantic landmarks on every page.
  • One H1 per page; logical heading order.
  • Full keyboard operation, with visible coral focus outlines.
  • Colour is never the only cue — text labels accompany every tag and chart.
  • Charts include hidden data tables; illustrations are marked decorative with text alternatives.
  • Respects prefers-reduced-motion; no autoplaying audio or video.
  • Content note on the story page, with a direct route to support.

Built as a static site — HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript, no frameworks or server — and hosted on GitHub Pages. Design tokens: warm paper #f3efe8, deep green #344f45, restrained coral #b7654a.
