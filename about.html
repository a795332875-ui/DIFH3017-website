How this site was made

This project was created for DIFH3017: Digital Humanities and Health. Below we document our method, our AI use, our privacy safeguards, and our accessibility choices — so that every decision can be inspected.

The brief: who, what, and why

Audience Hong Kong university students, roughly 18–24 years old, who may be experiencing stress but may not recognise the signals or feel able to seek help.
Objective Help readers recognise common patterns of stress, reduce the hesitation to reach out, and connect them to verified support — without diagnosing or labelling them.
Main message Stress often shows up in small, ordinary ways. Reaching out is a normal, brave step — not a sign of weakness or a diagnosis.
Scope (what's in) Fictional narrative, cited population evidence, verified support pathways, honest uncertainty, method & AI log.
Out of bounds No diagnosis, no treatment recommendations, no personal health data, no invented statistics, no real patient stories.
Safe next step Talk to a trusted person, contact a campus counsellor, or call a verified helpline.

AI use log

We used AI as a drafting assistant — not as an authority. Every AI output was inspected, verified, and edited by humans. Here is what AI helped with, and what humans kept control over.

HTML & CSS structure Drafted page layout, semantic structure, and CSS variables Inspected code; chose colour tokens; ensured semantic HTML (header, nav, main, section, footer).
Interactive story & choices Drafted story beats, choice options, and insight text Wrote and revised all narrative text; ensured Jay is clearly fictional; designed the "no score, no label" interaction so choices never diagnose or judge.
Evidence & sources Helped draft the source ledger table structure Verified all figures against cited sources; added denominators, limits, and uncertainty notes; removed any unverified claims.

AI did not decide the health message. Humans chose the topic, audience, evidence, limits, and support pathways. AI output was treated as a draft at every step.

Methodology

1. Evidence gathering

We prioritised Hong Kong-specific, aggregate, non-identifiable sources: the 2025 CUHK & Mental Health Association Depression Index Survey (N=2,695), a 5.5-year cohort study of local university students (Pan et al., 2025, N=960), and Hong Kong Department of Health figures. We did not collect, scrape, or analyse any personal data.

2. Story construction

Jay's day is a fictional composite, built from common patterns of student stress drawn from the local research — difficulty sleeping, trouble focusing, social withdrawal, and hesitation to seek help. He is not based on any real person. The story uses an interactive choice format, but choices never produce a score or label — each one opens a short, non-judgemental note about the signal it represents.

3. Evidence–story separation

The story and the statistics live on separate pages and are visually distinguished. Jay is explicitly labelled as fictional. The statistics are each given their source, population, measure, and denominator. We never present the story as evidence, nor the statistics as an explanation of any individual.

4. Verification

Every health claim was checked against a named source. Where we could not verify a figure's denominator or date, we did not include it. We note explicitly that the 5.5-year study ends in 2021 and the 2025 survey uses self-report screening (not clinical diagnosis).

Accessibility

We designed this site to be usable without a mouse, without sound, and with assistive technology. Below are the choices we made:

Structure & keyboard

  • Skip-to-content link at the top of every page.
  • Semantic HTML5 landmarks: header, nav, main, section, footer.
  • One H1 per page, logical heading hierarchy.
  • All interactive elements are keyboard-operable (Tab, Enter, Space).
  • Visible focus indicators (coral outline) on all focusable elements.

Visual design

  • Colour palette chosen for contrast: dark green ink on warm paper.
  • Colour is never the only way to convey meaning — text labels and tags accompany all colour cues.
  • Responsive layout that reflows on narrow screens.

Media & motion

  • No autoplaying audio or video. The story is fully readable as text.
  • The site respects the prefers-reduced-motion setting.
  • All images and visual elements have text equivalents. The storymap has a linear text fallback.

Content design

  • Plain language; short paragraphs; clear headings.
  • Content note on the story page with a skip route.
  • Support information is one click away from every page.

Ethical awareness

Health communication carries responsibility. Here is how we tried to get it right:

Person-first language

We avoid defining Jay by his struggles. He is a person experiencing stress, not "an anxious person" or "a depressive."

No stereotypes

The story avoids dramatic "crisis" tropes and shows an ordinary, relatable experience. Recovery is not a single turning point.

Honest uncertainty

We say what we don't know. We mark dated evidence. We separate population data from individual experience.

No over-promising

We do not claim this site can diagnose, treat, or cure. We direct readers to verified professional support.

Technical notes

This is a static website built with HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript — no frameworks, no databases, no server. It can be hosted for free on GitHub Pages or any static hosting service.

  • HTML: Semantic structure with ARIA labels and landmarks.
  • CSS: Custom properties for design tokens, mobile-first responsive layout, reduced-motion support.
  • JavaScript: Lightweight story navigation with ARIA live regions for screen reader announcements.
  • Design tokens: warm paper (#f3efe8), deep green (#344f45), restrained coral (#b7654a).
